Strong on the Benchmark, Broken on a New Vehicle

Open datasets and released models have made strong driving perception and planning widely accessible, yet a strong benchmark score does not make a released model ready for a real vehicle. Differences in sensor configuration, data distribution and execution process can invalidate what the model learned on its source system — and they persist even after the obvious adaptation is done.

R2R takes a released model and ordinary driving from the target vehicle, localizes what remains coupled to the source system by controlled intervention, and corrects only that part.

(a) A released model that scores well on benchmarks fails in perception and planning on a new vehicle in a new city. (b) Retraining would require collecting and annotating data for every sensor rig. (c) R2R localizes what remains coupled to the source system and corrects only that part: sensor adaptation with frozen weights and behavior adaptation with Human-Anchored RL (HA-RL).

Higher Open-Loop Score, Lower Closed-Loop Success

Conventional post-training — imitation learning followed by RL with a trajectory-level driving score — raises the planner's open-loop score from 0.698 to 0.809, the highest of all variants. In closed loop its success rate falls from 82.2% to 78.1%, and collisions rise from 4.4% to 12.2%.

One Methodology: Intervene → Localize → Correct

R2R varies one deployment variable while holding the remaining inputs, representations or parameters fixed, localizes the source dependency, and corrects only the affected objective, interface or representation. Both halves start from the same step: sensor-interface alignment (SIA) re-renders the recorded target cameras as the cameras each model was trained with.

Top, behavior adaptation: SIA, imitation learning on target driving, then HA-RL — the ego state follows the planner's own execution through a tracker on recorded observations, and the reward anchors it to human behavior. Bottom, sensor adaptation (training-free): spatial representation alignment (SRA) re-weights the source spatial prior of the frozen detector, and temporal representation alignment (TRA) restores the source frame interval in its temporal memory.

Abstract

Released autonomous-driving models achieve strong benchmark performance, yet deploying them on a new real vehicle remains challenging. We study this deployment gap and find that released models can remain coupled to assumptions from their source sensing, data, and execution processes even after conventional adaptation. We introduce Released-to-Real (R2R), a deployment-localization methodology that identifies such source-system coupling and corrects only the affected components while preserving the released model. For data and behavior adaptation, we find that the mismatch between trajectory-level optimization and receding-horizon execution enters through the ego state rather than the observation, and introduce Human-Anchored Reinforcement Learning (HA-RL), which corrects it on recorded target driving without the costly rollouts of generative closed-loop reinforcement learning (RL). For physical sensor adaptation, R2R localizes and corrects source-dependent spatial and temporal representations with frozen perception weights, requiring neither target annotations nor retraining. HA-RL improves the closed-loop success rate of a released diffusion-based planner from 82.2% to 95.2%, while sensor adaptation across multiple released detectors and three real sensor rigs improves the vehicle mean average precision (mAP) of a released multi-camera detector from 0.041 to 0.420 on a five-camera target rig. These results provide a practical path for preparing released models before deployment on new real vehicles.
